# Stage 1: Production Workflow
## Idea → Frequency-Aware → Client-Ready Demo

The goal of Stage 1 is NOT a rough sketch. It's a demo that a client hears and immediately says YES — because every instrument is already living in its own frequency space, complementing not clashing. Mixing then becomes creative polish, not problem-solving.

---

## ADI'S PERSONAL COMPOSITION METHOD

Adi composes **melody-first, structure-later**. Never impose a structure on him upfront.

### How He Works
1. **Piano → Melody** — starts at the piano (Casio CTK-7300IN or Yamaha DGX-660), plays until a melodic idea emerges. No labels — it's not "the chorus" yet, it's just a melody.
2. **15–16 Reference Tracks** — listens widely around the emotional target. Narrows to 3–5 tight references and builds a **Catalog of Attributes** (sonic/harmonic/rhythmic traits to use) + **Avoidance List** (genre clichés to skip). References go away before the DAW opens.
3. **Melody is fluid** — the same melody could become verse, chorus, pre-chorus, or get discarded and used as pure inspiration for timbre/feel.
4. **Instrument swapping generates the arrangement** — playing the melody on different instruments (piano → strings → synth → tumbi → bass) reveals which version becomes the hook, which becomes texture.

### The 8 Mutation Method (how one melody becomes a full arrangement)
- Duplicate the piano melody **8 times** in Logic/Ableton
- Change **one parameter only** per duplicate: instrument swap, rhythm shift, one chord change, register transposition
- This creates a **family of related ideas** — all variations of the same seed
- Assign each to the **3D Depth Chart**:

| Layer | Role | Adi's Instruments |
|---|---|---|
| **Foreground** | Hook — complex, fast, high | Tumbi riff, vocal melody, piano lead |
| **Midground** | Support — steady, medium-pitch | Synth arp, rhythm guitar, chord stabs |
| **Background** | Texture — slow, sustained, low | Omnisphere pad, bass root, drum groove |

### Sculpt Don't Build (arrangement approach)
- Stack ALL 8 mutation loops across the full timeline first
- Then **mute and carve** to create sections — verse = remove layers, chorus = restore them
- Structure emerges from subtraction, not planning

## STEP 1: LOCK THE FOUNDATION (Before adding anything)

Before placing a single instrument, establish:

**Key & Scale**
- Decide the key. Everything tuned to this — including 808s, bass, string voicings.
- Use Scaler 3 to explore chord progressions that serve the emotional intent.

**BPM & Feel**
- Lock BPM early. Tempo affects how reverb tails, delays, and rhythmic elements behave.
- Triplet/swing feel vs. straight — decide before programming drums.

**Emotional Intention**
- What is this track trying to make someone feel? (Sadness, power, euphoria, nostalgia?)
- Every instrument choice, voicing, and register decision flows from this.
- This is Adi's superpower — always start here.

**Reference Track**
- Pick one reference that nails the emotional target + sonic quality.
- Keep it in Logic and A/B against it throughout production.

## STEP 2: BUILD THE FREQUENCY ARCHITECTURE

Think of the frequency spectrum as a building. Each instrument gets a floor. No two instruments should fight for the same floor without one giving way.

### The Frequency Map (20Hz–20kHz)

```
SUB BASS (20–60Hz)
└── OWNER: Kick sub, 808 fundamental, bass fundamental
└── RULE: Only ONE element dominates here. Everything else HPF'd above it.

BASS (60–200Hz)
└── OWNER: Bass instrument (Session Bassist, synth bass), kick body
└── RULE: Bass plays the root. Keep it mostly MONO. Pads/strings — HPF here.

LOW-MIDS (200–500Hz)
└── OWNER: Warmth of piano, cello body, acoustic guitar body
└── DANGER ZONE: Most frequency clashes happen here. Be surgical.
└── RULE: Only 1–2 instruments should have presence here. Others cut it out.

MIDS (500Hz–2kHz)
└── OWNER: Melodic content — vocal, piano melody, lead synth, viola
└── RULE: Vocals own 800Hz–2kHz. Everything else ducks or EQ'd around vocal.

UPPER MIDS / PRESENCE (2–5kHz)
└── OWNER: Vocal clarity, guitar attack, violin brightness
└── RULE: Guard this for the lead element (usually vocal). Others roll off here.

AIR / BRILLIANCE (8–16kHz)
└── OWNER: Cymbals, string shimmer, reverb tails, vocal air
└── RULE: Shared space — but avoid harsh peaks. Smooth shelves only.

ULTRA HIGH (16–20kHz)
└── OWNER: Overhead mics, ambience, tape hiss (if stylistic)
└── RULE: Often filtered entirely — adds nothing musically below pro monitor level
```

## STEP 3: INSTRUMENT PLACEMENT PROTOCOL

For EVERY new instrument added, answer these 4 questions before committing:

**Q1: What register is it playing in?**
Low (below 250Hz) / Mid (250Hz–3kHz) / High (above 3kHz)?

**Q2: Who else lives in that register?**
List the instruments already there. Is there space or is it already occupied?

**Q3: How do I carve space for this instrument?**
- HPF to remove low end it doesn't need
- EQ cut the region where it overlaps with the existing occupant
- Transpose the voicing up or down to a less occupied register
- Change the articulation (e.g., pizzicato instead of sustained bow — different frequency weight)

**Q4: Does it complement or complete the frequency picture?**
The goal is a full-spectrum picture where each instrument is clearly audible without fighting.

## STEP 4: VOICING RULES BY INSTRUMENT TYPE

### Strings (Emotional Cello, Emotional Viola, Cinematic Studio Strings)
- **Cello:** Plays in bass/low-mid register (60–400Hz) — keep voicings in lower positions
- **Viola:** Mid register (200Hz–2kHz) — ideal for inner harmony and texture
- **Violins (CSS/Spitfire):** Upper mid to high (800Hz–5kHz) — melody or counter-melody
- **RULE:** Strings playing in the same register must be voiced a 3rd or more apart
- **HPF:** Always HPF cello at 60Hz, viola at 100Hz, violins at 150Hz — remove rumble

### Piano (Alicia's Keys, Claire, Noire)
- **Left hand:** Bass register — if bass guitar/808 present, mute left hand or HPF at 200Hz
- **Right hand:** Mid register — this is where it complements vocals
- **RULE:** If a vocal is present, piano should NOT play in the 800Hz–2kHz range aggressively
- **Voicing tip:** Use open voicings (spread chords) to reduce low-mid density

### Pads / Synths (Omnisphere 3, Serum 2, Zebra 3)
- Pads are texture — they should live BEHIND everything else
- HPF pads aggressively — usually 200–400Hz depending on density
- Automate pad volume to breathe with the arrangement (swell in chorus, back off in verse)
- Pads should fill the upper mid/air space (2–8kHz) — not compete with bass or vocal

### World Instruments (NI India, Tablas, Ethno World 6)
- Tabla: Mid-focused attack (200Hz–3kHz) — sidestep with kick at 60–80Hz
- Sitar/sarangi: Present in 800Hz–5kHz — watch against lead vocal
- Use these for rhythmic and melodic texture, not low-end foundation

### Drums / Percussion
- **Kick:** Sub (50–80Hz punch) + high transient (3–5kHz click) — these two areas define it
- **Snare:** Body at 150–250Hz + crack at 4–6kHz
- **Hi-hats/cymbals:** 6kHz+ — they live in the air zone
- **RULE:** If kick and bass are clashing sub, tune them — bass plays root, kick has its own sub note

## STEP 5: THE CLIENT-READY DEMO TEST

Before calling Stage 1 complete, the demo must pass these 5 checks:

### ✅ Check 1: The Mute Test
Mute each instrument one at a time. Can you clearly hear the "hole" it leaves?
If yes — it has its own space. If no — it's buried or redundant. Remove or reposition it.

### ✅ Check 2: The Translation Test
Play it through AirPods. Does it still make sense? Can you hear the melody, the bass, the vocal?
If something disappears — it doesn't have its own frequency territory.

### ✅ Check 3: The Emotion Test
Does it immediately hit the emotional target? Would a client/artist say YES in 10 seconds?
If not — the arrangement isn't serving the emotion yet. Go back to Step 1 intention.

### ✅ Check 4: The Frequency Stack Test
Open Pro-Q 4 on the master bus (analysis only, no processing). Look at the spectrum.
- Is there a clear low end (60–200Hz)?
- Is the mid range (500Hz–3kHz) full but not congested?
- Is there air (8kHz+)?
- Is there a massive buildup at 200–400Hz? (Most common problem — fix it here, not in mix)

### ✅ Check 5: The Dynamics Test
Does the track breathe? Is there a difference between verse and chorus energy?
Chorus should feel noticeably bigger — through layering, not just volume.

---

## STEP 6: GAIN STAGING FROM DAY ONE

Start as you mean to go on. Good gain staging in production = easier mixing later.

- Each instrument channel: peak no higher than **-12 to -18dBFS** before any plugins
- No plugins on the master bus yet (analysis only with Pro-Q 4 if needed)
- Keep the master output below -6dBFS at all times during production
- This headroom is your mixing space — protect it

## COMMON PRODUCTION MISTAKES TO AVOID

| Mistake | Why it's a problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Too many instruments in low-mids | Everything sounds muddy, nothing defined | HPF everything that doesn't own 200–500Hz |
| Bass playing full chords | Clashes with kick, blurs low end | Root notes only below 150Hz |
| Pads with no HPF | Eats up low-mid space, makes mix dense | HPF pads at 200–400Hz |
| Strings doubled in same register | Phase issues, comb filtering, mud | Transpose one part up an octave or a 3rd |
| All instruments at same volume | No front-to-back depth | Layer by importance: lead > support > texture |
| No automation in production | Track feels static, not alive | At minimum: pad swells, volume rides on key moments |
| Reference track ignored | Chasing a target you can't see | A/B every 20 minutes against reference |

## ADI'S PRODUCTION APPROACH — KEY PRINCIPLES

1. **Frequency first, creativity always** — know the rules so you can serve the emotion
2. **Every instrument needs a reason** — if it's not adding something specific, remove it
3. **The vocal is always the priority** — every production decision should serve the vocal's clarity
4. **Less is more in verses, more in choruses** — contrast is what makes choruses feel big
5. **Trust your emotional instinct** — if it feels wrong, it is wrong. Fix the technical, trust the feel.
